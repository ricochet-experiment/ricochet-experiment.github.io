### The Laue-Langevin Institute

Neutrinos have been postulated in 1930 by Wolfang Pauli to explain the spectrum of beta-decay. 
Since then, radioactive sources have been an extensively source of neutrinos, from their first detection to the measurement of key properties. 
Reactor neutrinos are particularly suitable to study neutrinos in the low-energy regime, while delivering an intense flux of particles. 
The Ricochet experiment is located at the [Laue-Langevin Institute](https://www.ill.eu/for-all-users/instruments/instruments-list/ricochet/description/instrument-layout), holding the only nuclear reactor in France entirely dedicated to scientific research.
The setup is installed at 8.8 m from the nuclear core, subject to a flux of 10¹² electron antineutrinos/cm²/s created during the fission process, previously been measured with precision by the [STEREO experiment](https://www.stereo-experiment.org/). 
The reactor is active during 3 cycles per year, summing to an average of six months, providing ideal conditions to characterize both the CEvNS signal and the environmental background.
